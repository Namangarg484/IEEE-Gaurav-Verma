\begin{abstract}
\textit{Objective:} Reported accuracies of physiological estimation from
photoplethysmography (PPG) are often inflated by data leakage that
subject-wise splitting does not detect.  We develop and validate an audit
that detects and measures such leakage.
\textit{Methods:} The audit combines a detector for label-construction
leaks, a copy probe, asserted split protocols, a leakage ladder that
measures inflation per mechanism, and a null-anchored decision rule with
subject-cluster bootstrap intervals and controls.  It was validated on
synthetic cohorts with known truth, used to re-audit a published
PPG$\to$glucose result, and applied to three glucose cohorts and two
blood-pressure cohorts (\m{bpNSubjects} and \m{vdNSubjects} subjects).
\textit{Results:} The detector found every corrupted subject for cycle
lengths $k\ge3$ without false alarms on \m{valRealNegSubjects} real subjects
and flagged \m{valRealPosFlagged}/\m{valRealPosEligible} patients of a public
glucose derivative.  The decision rule's false-claim rate stayed below the nominal
2.5\% under honest splits but reached \m{valNullLeakyRidgeRej}--\m{valNullLeakyGbmRej}\% under leaky ones.  A published accuracy (MAE 2.96~mg/dL) was matched (\m{raPubCgMAE})
only when augmentation preceded the split, where a nearest-neighbour
memoriser scored \m{raPubNnMAE}; under a subject split the model did not
beat the mean.  On VitalDB a leaky split lowered gradient-boosting MAE from
\m{vdSbpSubjGbmDemoMAE} to \m{vdSbpSegGbmDemoMAE}~mmHg, mostly through demographic
quasi-identifiers; a genuine calibration-free waveform signal remained.  No glucose
model, including PPG foundation models, beat the null by more than a
fraction of the reference noise, a bound limited by cohort size.
\textit{Conclusion:} Leakage-aware, null-anchored evaluation separates
physiological signal from artefact.
\textit{Significance:} The open audit lets wearable studies test for
leakage.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Photoplethysmography, data leakage, evaluation methodology, blood glucose,
blood pressure, cross-validation, wearable sensing, reproducibility.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{C}{uffless} estimation of blood pressure, glucose and other
physiological variables from photoplethysmography (PPG) is an active
engineering target, and reported errors often approach those of regulated
devices~\cite{soliman2024,syamsul2026,chen2024mvcft}.  Evaluation is the weak
link.  Record-wise splits of autocorrelated wearable signals inflate
accuracy~\cite{yoshizawa2023,mehta2024,cheung2024}, and recent re-evaluations
of PPG-based glucose estimation found that published models do not beat a
mean predictor once subjects are separated~\cite{ramesh2026,seyedebrahimi2026}.
Subject-wise splitting is now the recommended
remedy~\cite{saeb2017,collins2024tripod}, but it is not sufficient: leaks
introduced while a dataset is \emph{constructed} survive any split, and the
magnitude of a leak depends on the model as well as on the protocol.

This paper treats evaluation as an engineering problem with a method that can
itself be validated.  Its contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{An audit method} (Sec.~\ref{sec:method}) with four
        components: a detector for label-construction leaks with a block-scan
        variant for partial corruption, a copy probe for augmented or
        duplicated samples, split protocols whose no-leak guarantee is
        asserted in code before training, a leakage ladder that measures
        inflation mechanism by mechanism, and a null-anchored decision rule
        with controls.
  \item \textbf{Validation on known ground truth} (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}):
        detector sensitivity and specificity under injected corruption and
        its failure modes, and the false-claim rate and power of the decision
        rule under honest and leaky protocols.
  \item \textbf{Applications} (Sec.~\ref{sec:apps}).  For PPG$\to$glucose
        the audit uncovers a previously unreported label-replication artefact
        in a public derivative, bounds what three cohorts support, and
        re-audits a published result, matching its accuracy only under a
        copy leak.  For PPG$\to$blood pressure, on a 219-subject cohort and on
        \m{vdNSubjects} surgical patients, it recovers a genuine signal,
        measures how much a random split inflates it and through which
        inputs, and quantifies how often a signal of that size would be
        detected in cohorts as small as the glucose cohorts.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Audit Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Setting and leak taxonomy}
A dataset is a set of windows $w$, each with a signal segment, a reference
label $y$, a \emph{record} (one independent reference measurement), a
\emph{subject} and, for longitudinal data, a time $t$.  A split protocol
produces folds $(\mathcal{T}_f,\mathcal{E}_f)$.  We distinguish six leak
levels (Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}): window, record, subject and temporal leaks
arise from the split; copy leaks arise when augmented or duplicated samples
are created before it; label-construction leaks arise when a table pairs
signal rows with labels so that a label no longer belongs to the signal it
accompanies.  Kapoor and Narayanan's taxonomy~\cite{kapoor2023leakage}
covers the first four and copies under its duplicates type; label
construction is closest to duplicates but acts on the label column.

\subsection{Label-construction detector}
\label{sec:detector}
A cross-join of a subject's $k$ reference values with its signal rows produces
a label column with $y_i=y_{i-k}$ for almost all $i$, whereas a genuine table
is record-structured (runs of one label per record).  For a label column
$y_{1:n}$ we compute the lag-$\ell$ match fraction
$\phi(\ell)=\frac{1}{n-\ell}\sum_{i>\ell}\mathbb{1}[y_i=y_{i-\ell}]$ for
$\ell=1,\dots,\ell_{\max}$ and report the smallest $\ell$ with
$\phi(\ell)>\tau$ ($\tau=0.98$).  $\ell=1$ indicates runs (genuine); $\ell\ge2$
indicates cycling.  Because a corrupted stretch covering a fraction $p$ of a
subject's rows is diluted to $\phi(k)\approx p+(1-p)(1-k/m)$ for run length
$m$, the whole-column test misses partial corruption; the \emph{block scan}
applies the same test to contiguous blocks of $b$ rows (stride $b/2$).  On a
record-structured column any block is a single run or crosses a run boundary,
so $\phi(1)\ge1-1/b>\tau$ whenever $b>1/(1-\tau)$ and $b>\ell_{\max}$: the
block scan cannot raise a false alarm on genuine labels stored in record (or
acquisition) order, which the test therefore requires.  It cannot detect a
cross-join whose rows were subsequently shuffled; when record identifiers
exist, a complementary check that every record carries exactly one label
covers that case.

\subsection{Split protocols with asserted guarantees}
Each protocol declares the leak level it removes (Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}).
Before any model is trained, the folds are checked by exact set arithmetic on
window, record and subject identifiers and, for the temporal level, by the
minimum distance between a test reading and any training reading of the same
subject.  A violation aborts the run.  Folds are frozen to files with a
fingerprint so a third-party model is scored on identical folds.

\subsection{Leakage ladder}
The same model is evaluated under a sequence of protocols that leak
progressively less (random windows $\to$ random records $\to$ subjects; or
random readings $\to$ chronological $\to$ chronological with guard gap).  The
difference in a metric between adjacent rungs measures the inflation due to
one mechanism, separately from the others.

\subsection{Null-anchored decision rule and controls}
\label{sec:rule}
Window predictions are averaged per record.  Every model is compared with the
null appropriate to the use case: the training mean for cross-subject
estimation; each subject's training mean, a time-of-day model and persistence
for within-subject tracking.  The paired MAE difference $\Delta$ is bootstrapped
by resampling \emph{subjects} with replacement (5\,000 draws for the cohort
analyses, 2\,000 inside the simulations, the re-audit and the VitalDB grid),
and a model is declared better than the null only if the 95\% interval of
$\Delta$ lies entirely below zero.  A model trained with several seeds is
judged on its seed-ensemble prediction (mean of the seeds' predictions per
record); per-seed results are reported alongside.  One comparison per cohort is pre-specified as primary;
the others are exploratory.  Positive controls (quantities that must be
recoverable, e.g.\ heart rate) and negative controls (permuted labels,
noise input) are run through the identical pipeline, and the reference
device's own error (ISO~15197 meter, CGM MARD) is reported as a noise floor.
A \emph{copy probe}, a 1-nearest-neighbour regressor on the raw
(z-scored) signal, is run wherever copies are plausible.  It beats the null
when a test sample has a close raw-signal neighbour with a similar label in
training: an augmented or duplicated copy, or, when labels are
subject-specific, a near-identical recording of the same subject.  It is a
targeted check for copies, not a general leak detector: it misses
time-shifted overlapping windows of quasi-periodic PPG (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation})
and identity leaks that act through features, both of which the ladder
measures.

\section{Validation on Known Ground Truth}
\label{sec:validation}

\textbf{Detector.}  We generated label columns for 20 subjects, corrupted a
fraction (10--100\%) of them with cross-joins of cycle length $k\in\{2,3,5,7,10\}$
over 25, 50 or 100\% of their rows, and left the rest record-structured.
Fig.~\ref{fig:validation}a shows subject-level sensitivity.  Both detectors
had \m{valLocalMinSpec}\% specificity in every condition.  The whole-column
test detected fully corrupted subjects (sensitivity $\ge$\m{valGlobalFullSens}\%)
but missed partial corruption for long cycles (minimum \m{valGlobalPartialMinSens}\%);
the block scan detected every corrupted subject for $k\ge3$
(\m{valLocalMinSensKThree}\%) and at least \m{valLocalPartialMinSens}\% overall,
the residual misses at $k=2$ being draws in which the two reference values
coincide and the cycle is invisible.  On the label columns of
\m{valRealNegSubjects} real subjects from three clean cohorts it raised
\m{valRealNegFlagged} alarms; on the public glucose derivative of
Sec.~\ref{sec:glucose} it flagged \m{valRealPosFlagged} of the
\m{valRealPosEligible} patients with more than one reference value.  The
clean synthetic columns are record-ordered by construction, so their
specificity follows from Sec.~\ref{sec:detector}; we therefore also stressed
the two assumptions.  A cross-join whose rows were shuffled was detected in
\m{valShuffledMaxSens}\% of draws, and a genuine table stored window-major
(window~1 of every record, then window~2, \dots) raised a false alarm whenever
it had at most \m{valWindowMajorMaxRecords} records; the same tables in record
order raised none.  The detector is therefore valid only on rows in record or
acquisition order.

\textbf{Decision rule.}  We generated \m{valReps} synthetic cohorts per
condition (16 subjects, 12 records of three 5-s windows each).  Each label is
a subject offset (SD 20~mg/dL, unlearnable from the signal) plus $g$ times
the heart-rate deviation encoded in the waveform plus reference noise.  Each
subject also has a pulse-shape fingerprint unrelated to the label, so a
flexible model can learn identity, and hence the offset, whenever a protocol
lets a subject's windows into training.  Fig.~\ref{fig:validation}b shows how
often the rule declared a ridge or a gradient-boosting (GBM) model better
than the train-mean null (4-fold subject-wise folds; the leaky arm also
contains overlapping windows).  With no signal ($g=0$, \m{valNullReps}
additional cohorts) the honest false-claim rate was \m{valNullHonestRidgeRej}\%
for ridge (exact 95\% CI \m{valNullHonestRidgeRejLo}--\m{valNullHonestRidgeRejHi}\%)
and \m{valNullHonestGbmRej}\% for GBM (\m{valNullHonestGbmRejLo}--\m{valNullHonestGbmRejHi}\%),
below the nominal one-sided 2.5\%: the rule is conservative.  Under the leaky protocol it
rose to \m{valNullLeakyRidgeRej}\% (ridge) and \m{valNullLeakyGbmRej}\% (GBM), the
GBM \emph{appearing} to improve on the null by \m{valLeakyGbmGZeroDiff}~mg/dL
with no signal present: the rule is valid only together with an asserted
protocol.  With signal present the honest rule's power rose with $g$, to
\m{valHonestRidgeGFourRej}\% (ridge) and \m{valHonestGbmGFourRej}\% (GBM) at
$g=4$, but was only \m{valHonestRidgeGOneRej}\% and \m{valHonestGbmGOneRej}\% at
$g=1$: with 16 subjects small effects go undetected.  The generator has no
confounder linking a nuisance variable to the label, a case the rule cannot
distinguish from signal and the controls must address.

\textbf{Copy probe.}  On the Kermani--Esmaeili random-window rung, where
overlapping windows of one recording cross the split, the Euclidean probe did
not beat the null ($\Delta$ \m{cpWinEucDiff}~mg/dL, \m{cpWinEucDiffCI}) and a
shift-invariant variant (maximum normalised cross-correlation) only
approached it (\m{cpWinShiftDiff}, \m{cpWinShiftDiffCI}): quasi-periodic pulses
correlate with any other pulse at some lag, so time-shifted copies do not
stand out.  The probe is decisive for augmented copies (Sec.~\ref{sec:reaudit})
and is not a substitute for the ladder.

\section{Applications}
\label{sec:apps}

\subsection{Data and models}
\textbf{Glucose.}  Kermani--Esmaeili finger PPG~\cite{kermani2023data}
(\m{mendNRecords} 10-s recordings, \m{mendNSubjects} subjects, fingerstick
reference); BIG~IDEAs wrist BVP with Dexcom~G6 CGM~\cite{cho2026bigideas,bent2021}
(\m{bigNSubjects} subjects, \m{bigNRecordsAll} readings, HbA1c
\m{bigHbAOneCMin}--\m{bigHbAOneCMax}\%); and STUBA ePPG~\cite{stuba2026}
(\m{stubaNRecords} records, \m{stubaNSubjects} subjects, \m{stubaNDiabetic} with
type-1 diabetes).  \textbf{Blood pressure.}  PPG-BP~\cite{liang2018ppgbp}:
\m{bpNSubjects} subjects, one cuff reading and three 2.1-s finger-PPG
segments each; VitalDB~\cite{lee2022vitaldb}: 10-s segments with
beat-wise arterial-line labels from surgical cases (Sec.~\ref{sec:bp}).
Signals are band-passed 0.5--8~Hz, resampled to 64~Hz and
z-scored per window (5~s for glucose).  Models: nulls; ridge and gradient
boosting on nine hand-crafted PPG features, with and without demographics;
a 1-D CNN, 2-D wavelet-scalogram CNN, Takens-embedding PointNet and their
cross-attention fusion~\cite{takens1981,qi2017pointnet,vaswani2017attention};
re-implemented ResNet-1D and CNN--LSTM~\cite{zeynali2025,syamsul2026}; and
ridge probes on frozen embeddings of three PPG foundation models
(PaPaGei-S~\cite{papagei2025}, Pulse-PPG~\cite{pulseppg2025},
AnyPPG~\cite{anyppg2026}).  Networks use one shared configuration (AdamW,
fixed budget, no early stopping on held-out data) and three seeds, except on
VitalDB, where every model was run once.
Protocols: leave-one-subject-out (fingerstick cohorts), chronological 70/30
with a 1-h guard gap (BIG~IDEAs; plus a personalised arm trained on the
residual to each subject's training mean), 5-fold subject-wise (PPG-BP,
VitalDB), and for VitalDB also random-segment and a calibration-based
personal split (first half of each case trains, second half tests).
Full tables are in the supplement.

\subsection{PPG $\to$ glucose}
\label{sec:glucose}
\textbf{Label-construction leak.}  A widely shared Kaggle derivative of the
Kermani--Esmaeili recordings~\cite{kaggle_ppg} cycles each patient's
reference values row by row (Fig.~\ref{fig:artifact}), which the detector
flags for every patient with more than one value.  Every window of that file
inherits the patient mean, so a per-patient oracle scores MAE
\m{derivOracleMAE}~mg/dL with $r=\m{derivOracleR}$ under a random split,
and a correct subject-wise split cannot reveal the problem.  An earlier,
unpublished draft of this work trained on the file and reported MAE
6.89~mg/dL; those numbers are not carried forward.

\textbf{Ladder.}  On the raw recordings the 1-D CNN scores
\m{mendLadderWindowOneD}~mg/dL ($r=\m{mendLadderWindowOneDR}$) under random
windows, but \m{mendLadderRecordOneD} and \m{mendLadderSubjectOneD} once
recordings or subjects are separated, against a null of \m{mendMeanMAE}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ladder}a): on this cohort window-neighbour leakage, not
identity leakage, produces the inflation, because most subjects contribute
one or two recordings.  On BIG~IDEAs the ladder is nearly flat
(\m{bigLadderReadingOneD}, \m{bigLadderChronoNoGapOneD},
\m{bigLadderChronoOneD}~mg/dL) and every rung stays above the subject-mean null.

\textbf{Bounds.}  Table~\ref{tab:glucose} summarises the honest protocols.
On the fingerstick cohort the pre-specified primary comparison (1-D CNN)
gives $\Delta$ \m{mendOneDDiff}~mg/dL (95\%~CI \m{mendOneDDiffCI}).  By the
decision rule this is a positive result, but a fragile and small one: the

the personalised arm the best model, a Pulse-PPG probe, reaches $\Delta$

the CGM noise floor (\m{bigNoiseFloorMAE}~mg/dL); median within-subject
correlation stays below 0.2 for every model.  On STUBA no model beats the
null.  No PPG model detects any of the \m{bigHypoN}+\m{stubaHypoNAll}
hypoglycaemic references.  Positive controls behave as required: heart rate
is recovered from the same windows ($r=\m{ctlHrR}$), while permuted labels
and noise inputs return to the null.

\subsection{Re-audit of a published claim}
\label{sec:reaudit}
Soliman \emph{et~al.}~\cite{soliman2024} report MAE 2.96~mg/dL, $R^2=0.97$
and 100\% Clarke~A for a CNN--GRU on the same 67 Kermani--Esmaeili
recordings.  The paper states a 0.5--8~Hz band-pass, downsampling to 30~Hz,
Gaussian-noise augmentation from 67 to 269 samples, shuffling and 10-fold
cross-validation.  Its wording points both ways on the order of augmentation
and split: noise is added ``to the training PPG signals'', yet ``the sample
numbers are increased from 67 to 269'', a count of the whole dataset.  The
split unit, the noise levels and the layer sizes are not stated, and the
headline values do not match the paper's own per-fold table (fold MAE
0.77--3.82~mg/dL).  The re-audited result is an arXiv preprint.  We
reproduced the stated elements with a re-implementation of the described
architecture (two Conv1D branches and a GRU branch, each followed by three
dense layers; the GRU reads the sequence decimated to 10~Hz for speed),
generated three noisy copies per signal (67$\to$268 samples; noise SD 0.05,
0.10 and 0.20 of the signal SD), and resolved the unstated choices both ways
(Table~\ref{tab:reaudit}).  Early stopping uses a validation set drawn like
the test set: samples in~A, whole recordings or subjects, held out before
augmentation, in~B and~C.  Alongside the null we ran the copy probe of
Sec.~\ref{sec:rule}.

With augmentation before a sample-level split (protocol~A), the
re-implementation matches or exceeds the claim: MAE \m{raPubCgMAE}~mg/dL,
$R^2=\m{raPubCgRtwo}$, \m{raPubCgZoneA}\% Clarke~A, and it does so at half and
double our noise level (MAE \m{raNoiseHalfCgMAE} and \m{raNoiseDoubleCgMAE}).  The memoriser does even
better (MAE \m{raPubNnMAE}, $R^2=\m{raPubNnRtwo}$), because every test sample
has noisy siblings of the same recording in training.  Confining augmentation
to the training folds and splitting by recording (B) or by subject (C) removes
the copies, and the same network falls to \m{raRecCgMAE} and
\m{raSubjCgMAE}~mg/dL against nulls of \m{raRecNullMAE} and
\m{raSubjNullMAE}.  Under the subject split the seed-ensemble does not beat
the null ($\Delta$ \m{ensRaSubjCgDiff}~mg/dL, \m{ensRaSubjCgDiffCI}); its point
estimate is worse than the null in every seed, and in \m{raSubjCgNSigWorse} of
\m{raSubjSeeds} seeds significantly so.  The copy probe, now without copies,
is worse still.  The audit thus attributes
the published accuracy to a copy leak created by augmenting before splitting,
which the reported metrics cannot reveal and which any split that keeps a
recording's copies together removes.  We cannot verify the authors' exact pipeline,
whose unstated choices we had to resolve; the memoriser shows that protocol~A
admits near-perfect scores without any physiological signal.

\subsection{PPG $\to$ blood pressure}
\label{sec:bp}
\textbf{PPG-BP.}  Under the honest subject-wise split the 1-D CNN (seed
ensemble) estimates systolic pressure with MAE \m{ensBpSbpOneDMAE} against
\m{bpSbpSubjMeanMAE}~mmHg for the null ($\Delta$ \m{ensBpSbpOneDDiff},
\m{ensBpSbpOneDDiffCI}; $r=\m{bpSbpSubjOneDR}$), and ridge on PPG features alone
also clears the null ($\Delta$ \m{bpSbpSubjRidgeDiff}, \m{bpSbpSubjRidgeDiffCI})
(Table~\ref{tab:bp}).  On this cohort, however, age, sex, height and weight
alone do nearly as well ($\Delta$ \m{bpSbpSubjDemoDiff}, \m{bpSbpSubjDemoDiffCI}), and
adding the PPG features to them does not help (\m{bpSbpSubjRidgeDemoMAE} vs.\
\m{bpSbpSubjDemoMAE}~mmHg): PPG-BP shows that the rule detects
blood-pressure information, not that the information is specific to the
waveform.  Diastolic pressure is weaker; only the CNN clears the null
($\Delta$ \m{ensBpDbpOneDDiff}, \m{ensBpDbpOneDDiffCI}).

A random split over segments places a subject's other segments, and hence
its label, in training.  Ridge barely changes
(\m{bpSbpSegRidgeDemoMAE} vs.\ \m{bpSbpSubjRidgeDemoMAE}~mmHg); gradient boosting
on PPG features alone improves from \m{bpSbpSubjGbmMAE} to \m{bpSbpSegGbmMAE},
and with demographics, which nearly identify a subject, from
\m{bpSbpSubjGbmDemoMAE} to \m{bpSbpSegGbmDemoMAE}~mmHg ($r$ \m{bpSbpSubjGbmDemoR}
to \m{bpSbpSegGbmDemoR}).  Inflation thus grows with model capacity and with
quasi-identifiers among the inputs, and has to be measured for the model
being reported.  The copy probe does not beat the null under either split
($\Delta$ \m{bpSbpSegNnDiff}, \m{bpSbpSegNnDiffCI} on random segments): segments
of one recording start at different phases of the pulse and are not copies.

\textbf{VitalDB.}  PPG-BP has one reading per subject, which limits identity
leakage to three segments.  We therefore drew 600 random surgical cases of
VitalDB~\cite{lee2022vitaldb}, an open source behind PulseDB~\cite{wang2023pulsedb}
and many cuffless-BP results, and kept \m{vdNSegments} quality-controlled 10-s
finger-PPG segments from the \m{vdNCases} cases (\m{vdNSubjects} patients)
that yielded any, each labelled with the median beat systolic peak and
diastolic trough of the arterial line.  Benchmarks that enforce subject-wise
folds exist~\cite{gonzalez2023bpbench}, but segment-level splits remain
common~\cite{yoshizawa2023}.  Table~\ref{tab:vitaldb} gives the systolic
results (diastolic in the supplement).  Here demographics carry no
information ($\Delta$ \m{vdSbpSubjDemoDiff}~mmHg), yet with patients separated
the 1-D CNN beats the null ($\Delta$ \m{vdSbpSubjOneDDiff}, \m{vdSbpSubjOneDDiffCI};
$r=\m{vdSbpSubjOneDR}$): a calibration-free signal carried by the waveform.

The leaky random-segment split, which places minutes-apart segments of the
same surgery on both sides, leaves ridge unchanged (\m{vdSbpSubjRidgeMAE} vs.\
\m{vdSbpSegRidgeMAE}~mmHg) and gradient boosting on PPG features nearly so
(\m{vdSbpSubjGbmMAE} vs.\ \m{vdSbpSegGbmMAE}), but gradient boosting with
demographics falls from \m{vdSbpSubjGbmDemoMAE} to \m{vdSbpSegGbmDemoMAE}~mmHg
and its correlation doubles (\m{vdSbpSubjGbmDemoR} to \m{vdSbpSegGbmDemoR}).
Demographics that predict nothing across patients identify them within the
split, and the leak runs almost entirely through them.  It also reverses the
ranking of the two flexible models: on the leaky split gradient boosting
beats the CNN (\m{vdSbpSegGbmDemoMAE} vs.\ \m{vdSbpSegOneDMAE}), with patients
separated the CNN wins (\m{vdSbpSubjOneDMAE} vs.\ \m{vdSbpSubjGbmDemoMAE}).  The
copy probe stays above the null under every split, so the leak is an
identity leak through features, visible on the ladder but not to the probe.
For diastolic pressure the leak brings gradient boosting with demographics
to an error SD of \m{vdDbpSegGbmDemoSD}~mmHg, close to the 8-mmHg criterion of
ISO~81060-2, which we use only as a reference scale (cuffless devices fall
under IEEE~1708~\cite{ieee1708}); with patients separated its SD is
\m{vdDbpSubjGbmDemoSD}~mmHg.  A split error alone can make a model look nearly
compliant with a device standard.  Under the calibration-based protocol,
where models learn each case's deviation from its own training mean, all
models improve on that mean, but by little more than a millimetre of mercury
(best: 1-D CNN, $\Delta$ \m{vdSbpPersResOneDDiff}~mmHg, \m{vdSbpPersResOneDDiffCI}).

\textbf{Power at glucose-cohort sizes.}  Drawing 23 VitalDB patients with
three segments each (the Kermani--Esmaeili design) from the honest
predictions, the rule detected the CNN's systolic signal in \m{powVdSbpKE}\%
of draws; with 16 patients and all their segments (the BIG~IDEAs design) in
\m{powVdSbpBig}\%, and with 23 PPG-BP subjects in \m{powBpSbpKE}\%.  A signal of
blood-pressure size would often be missed in the glucose cohorts, so their
negative results bound the effect, by the minimum detectable effect, rather
than prove its absence.

\section{Discussion}
\textbf{What the audit adds.}  Subject-wise splitting, the standard remedy,
did not detect the largest problem we found: a label-construction leak in a
public table, which the detector finds in seconds from the label column
alone, provided the rows are in acquisition order.  Nor does it address
copies made before the split: the re-audited claim was matched only under
such a copy leak, which the copy probe exposes directly.  The ladder
separates mechanisms that are usually conflated: on the glucose recordings
window overlap, not identity, inflated the result; on PPG-BP and VitalDB
identity leakage inflated flexible models, mostly through demographic
inputs that act as quasi-identifiers, and on VitalDB it reversed which
model looked best.  Because the inflation depends on the model and its
inputs, a protocol cannot be certified once for all models; the ladder has
to be run for the model being reported.  Under honest protocols the
null-anchored rule kept its false-claim rate below nominal in simulation; on
real data it declared the blood-pressure signals and, narrowly, the
pre-specified Kermani--Esmaeili glucose comparison, an effect a fifth of
the meter's own error.  Ramesh \emph{et~al.}~\cite{ramesh2026} released a
re-implementation pipeline with participant-aware splits; the audit differs
in asserting each protocol's guarantee in code, detecting construction and
copy leaks, measuring inflation per mechanism, and validating its own
decision rule.

\textbf{What the applications show.}  A 2-s (PPG-BP) or 10-s (VitalDB)
finger-PPG segment carries calibration-free information about systolic
blood pressure, which on VitalDB is not explained by demographics.  For
glucose, every architecture tested, including frozen foundation models,
showed at most a weak and protocol-dependent cross-subject edge and no
measurable within-subject tracking, and no PPG model detected a hypo- or
hyperglycaemic reading.  Because a blood-pressure-sized signal would often
be missed at the glucose cohorts' sizes, these are bounds on the effect in
small, largely non-diabetic public cohorts, not proof that PPG carries no
glucose information.

\textbf{Recommendations.}  Run a construction-leak check on every derived
table, in acquisition order; augment only after splitting and run a copy
probe; name the protocol and the leak it removes, and freeze folds; measure
the ladder for the reported model, with and without quasi-identifying
inputs; report a null and the reference noise floor next to every result,
with subject-cluster intervals and a pre-specified primary comparison; and
include positive controls.

\textbf{Limitations.}  The re-audit reproduces the stated elements of a
preprint's pipeline, not the authors' code; its unstated choices were
resolved both ways, and only one claim was re-audited.  VitalDB patients
were anaesthetised and often received vasoactive drugs, so its
blood-pressure dynamics differ from ambulatory use; its quality control has
no arterial-damping check, missing demographics were mean-imputed, and its
models were run with one seed.  The detector targets order-preserving
cycling; shuffled cross-joins need the record-label check, and other
construction errors (e.g.\ shifted joins) need their own tests.  The copy
probe misses time-shifted copies of quasi-periodic signals.  The synthetic
validation is moderate in size and has no confounders.  Networks shared one
configuration; encoder probes were linear and used one common
preprocessing.  The glucose cohorts contain few diabetic subjects and few
hypoglycaemic references.

% =============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
We presented an audit for leakage in wearable physiological regression and
validated its components on known ground truth, including their failure
modes.  Applied to public PPG data it uncovered a label-construction leak in
a glucose derivative, attributed a published glucose accuracy to a copy
leak, measured how a random split inflates and reorders cuffless
blood-pressure models on \m{vdNSubjects} surgical patients, mostly through
quasi-identifying inputs, while confirming a genuine waveform signal, and
bounded the glucose information in small public cohorts.  The
implementation, frozen folds and all predictions are released so that any
new claim can be audited the same way.
